Method, data and caveats
  • FDI: DBT Inward Investment Results 2025-26 (Tables 4.1, 4.12). Published only for London's five ITL2 sub-areas, so each borough carries its sub-area's figures.
  • Deprivation: MHCLG English Indices of Deprivation 2025. "Most deprived 20%" = the 998 most deprived of London's 4,994 neighbourhoods (London-relative ranking).
  • Suppressed DBT values ([c]) are left blank, never zero; 2024-25 is the only year all five sub-areas are published. Projects per 100 neighbourhoods approximates a per-head rate (~1,500 people each).
  • Counts single-site projects only. Association, not causation.

Section 2

Is FDI housing the people it attracts?

Inner London takes 86% of FDI projects and 56% of migrants from abroad, but only 29% of approved new-home schemes. Outer London approves about 3× more new-home schemes per new migrant (20.8 vs 7.0 per 1,000). Inner West has the highest approval rate (80%), so the gap is fewer schemes coming forward, not more refusals. Canary Wharf cluster (5 Tower Hamlets wards): 6 of 22 new-home applications approved, 2021-25.

Method, data and caveats
  • Migration: ONS Census 2021 MIG001 (address one year before the census, during COVID-19). New migrants = from abroad + rest of UK (new to London).
  • Planning: London planning applications 2021-25 (foundations_london_housing dataset, 243,926 rows). New-home schemes (9,089) identified from descriptions, excluding follow-ups and householder extensions; development-corporation applications placed by coordinates.
  • Counts schemes, not homes (dwelling numbers are filled for under 1 in 10 schemes). Council portals record differently, so rates are more reliable than raw counts.
  • Housing-type figures come from words in descriptions, not official tenure data. Association, not causation.
